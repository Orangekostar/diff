# 6 Conclusions

State-dependent acquisition connects the choice of internal observations to the evolving compression-after-impact assessment. The method combines surface priors, acquired C-scan descriptors and the current strength estimate to select subsequent cells. A separate frozen predictor keeps the assessment mapping common across strategies, while the learning objective values prediction quality throughout the acquisition budget and at its endpoint.

Offline replay on 50 validation specimens produced a main-policy MAE of 44.286 MPa under the 25% cap, compared with 46.910 MPa for geometry-spread. Relative to surface-conditioned open-loop acquisition, the largest positive timing contribution difference occurred in the second completion stage. This locates the observed advantage beyond initialization, while later negative differences show that improvement was not sustained at every stage. Component comparisons remained mixed, and partial acquisition did not reach the 41.690 MPa complete-input MAE.

The framework characterizes how acquired subsets and their availability over time shape CAI prediction quality. Same-cap comparisons, signed timing contributions and matched-quality requirements provide complementary views of that relationship. The results are conditional on validation-based checkpoint selection and one policy-seed panel; they describe image-acquisition trade-offs rather than independently validated physical inspection savings.
